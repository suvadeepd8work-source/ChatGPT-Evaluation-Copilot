# Quantitative Survey Analysis: AI Trust & Verification

## 1. User Segmentation by Trust
The survey data (N=33) shows a cautious user base.
- **Skeptical Majority (81.8%)**: Combining those who believe AI should never be fully trusted (33.3%) and those who see it as useful but requiring verification (48.5%).
- **Optimists (12.1%)**: A small segment that views AI as mostly reliable.
- **The Unsure (6.1%)**: Users who are still calibrating their trust levels.

## 2. Verification vs. Blind Trust
### Verification Behavior
- **Active Verifiers (63.6%)**: Users who "Always" or "Often" verify AI outputs.
- **Passive Users (36.4%)**: Users who verify only "Sometimes," "Rarely," or "Never."

### Blind Trust Risk
- **High-Risk Tendency (48.5%)**: Nearly half of the respondents admit to "Frequently" or "Sometimes" submitting AI-generated work without a full review. This highlights a significant gap between *intent* (wanting to verify) and *action* (skipping review due to pressure or confidence).

## 3. High-Risk Usage Categories
Users are most vigilant in scenarios where inaccuracies have direct consequences:
1. **Research & Facts (63.6%)**: The top area where users feel verification is mandatory.
2. **High-Stakes Decisions (48.5%)**: Critical personal or professional choices.
3. **Career-related Work (45.4%)**: Emails, reports, and professional tasks.
4. **Academic/Financial/Legal (36.4% each)**: Structured fields with strict accuracy requirements.

## 4. Most Requested Evaluation Features
The data points toward a need for **External Validation** and **Human Oversight**:
- **Fact-checking Support (42.4%)**: The #1 requested feature.
- **Human Review Suggestions (33.3%)**: Users want the system to tell them *when* a human needs to step in.
- **Alternative Viewpoints (30.3%)**: Breaking the AI's "single-path" narrative.
- **Source Citations (24.2%)**: Grounding answers in reality.
- **Confidence Indicators (21.2%)**: Visual cues for AI uncertainty.

## 5. Barriers to Verification
The primary obstacles preventing users from verifying are:
- **Time Pressure**: The leading reason for skipping reviews.
- **The "Convincing" Tone**: Users admit that when an answer *sounds* right, they are less likely to check it.
- **Lack of Expertise**: Not knowing *how* to verify complex technical or legal data.
