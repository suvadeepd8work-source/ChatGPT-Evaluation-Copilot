# Key Findings: Qualitative Analysis Summary

## 1. The "Fluency Trap" is Universal
Regardless of age or occupation, users initially struggle with "Automation Bias." The professional formatting and confident tone of LLMs are the primary barriers to critical thinking.

## 2. Verification is a "Spectrum"
Users do not verify everything equally. They use a mental "Risk vs. Effort" calculation. Evaluation Mode must lower the *effort* of verification to match the *risk* level.

## 3. Transparency > Politeness
Experienced users value "Chain of Thought" and "Admission of Limits" more than polite or conversational filler. The system should prioritize exposing the AI's internal reasoning.

## 4. The Need for "Active" Grounding
Users are no longer satisfied with just "Citations." They demand "Verified Citations"—proof that the source exists and actually contains the claimed information.

## 5. Decision-Making is Domain-Specific
Trust is highest when the AI's logic matches the user's domain knowledge. Trust is lowest (and most critical) when the AI provides info outside the user's expertise.

## 6. The "Human-in-the-Loop" Mandate
Users want to be assisted, not replaced. The most successful evaluation features will be those that empower the user to make the final "Sign-off" with confidence.
