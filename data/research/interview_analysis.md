# Qualitative Interview Analysis: Deep Dive into User Trust

## 1. Overview
This analysis synthesizes qualitative data from 7 in-depth interviews across various demographics (Students, Tax Analysts, Software Engineers, IT Consultants). The goal is to understand the "Human-AI Trust Relationship" and how it impacts decision-making.

## 2. Trust Breakdown Moments
Users identified specific triggers that cause them to lose faith in AI:
- **Discovered Inaccuracy**: "Later I found some points were inaccurate" (Soumya).
- **Echo-Chambering**: "When it sounds to be answering based on only my response, and not on what's correct" (Shwetlana).
- **Subtle Logic Flaws**: "Logical flaws in code that look correct at first glance but fail during execution" (Sanchita).
- **Outdated/Inaccurate Data**: "Realizing it contained errors or outdated information" (Navarup).

## 3. Emotional Reactions to AI Outputs
- **Initial Comfort/Reliance**: "It feels natural to trust them at first" because of the confident tone.
- **Automation Bias**: A "safe" but dangerous feeling where the AI "sounded like it knew what it was talking about."
- **Professional Validation**: High trust when AI "matches my manual reviews" (Subhojyoti).
- **Skepticism from Over-Elaboration**: Users feel suspicious when AI is "too elaborative" or provides "lengthy descriptions" (Subhojyoti, Debapriya).

## 4. Decision-Making Patterns
- **The "Logic Match"**: Users trust AI when the reasoning aligns with their pre-existing domain knowledge.
- **The "High-Stakes" Pivot**: Users who are normally relaxed (Soumya, Suvamita) suddenly become rigorous when money, grades, or career reputation are involved.
- **The "Citation Anchor"**: For researchers (Neha, Shwetlana), the presence of citations is the primary decision point for trust.

## 5. Expectations from Evaluation Mode
Users want the system to do the "heavy lifting" of verification:
- **Verified Sources**: "I would trust AI more if it provided verified sources" (Soumya).
- **Real-time Verifiability**: "Built-in fact-checking that cross-references live, reputable sources" (Sanchita).
- **Transparency**: "Greater transparency, source citations" (Navarup).
- **Deep Dig Analysis**: Moving beyond surface-level summaries to actual technical depth (Subhojyoti, Debapriya).
