# Behavioral Patterns: Overreliance & Verification

## 1. Overreliance Patterns
- **The "Copy-Paste" Habit**: Students (Riya, Soumya) admit to using AI outputs in assignments without deep checking, often realizing mistakes only after submission.
- **Automation Bias**: A natural tendency to trust AI because it "sounds like it knows what it's talking about" (Sanchita).
- **Prompt Dependency**: Users like Shwetlana notice AI often "answers based only on my response" rather than objective truth.

## 2. Verification Behaviors
| Behavior | Trigger | Target Users |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Manual Cross-Check** | High-stakes work, academic assignments. | Arjun, Navarup, Suvamita |
| **Comparison to News** | Serious work, non-matching information. | Debapriya |
| **Total Verification** | Every output is double-checked against manual work. | Subhojyoti |
| **Citation Hunting** | Research and analysis. | Neha, Shwetlana |

## 3. Emotional Dependency Pattern
- **Initial Comfort**: AI's politeness and fluency create a "safe" feeling that leads to lower skepticism.
- **Frustration Loop**: Trust is high until a major error occurs, leading to a temporary "Skepticism Spike" before returning to high trust.
- **Authority Projection**: Users project human-like "knowledge" onto the model, forgetting it's a statistical predictor.

## 4. Skepticism Triggers
- **Volatile Data**: Niche topics or real-time news (Sanchita).
- **Complex Logic**: Long-form math or coding architecture (Sanchita).
- **Financial/Legal Stakes**: Anything involving money or law (Soumya).
