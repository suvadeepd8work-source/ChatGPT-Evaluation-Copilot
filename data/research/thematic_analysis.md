# Thematic Analysis: Recurring Qualitative Themes

## Theme 1: The "Fluency vs. Fact" Conflict
The most dominant theme across all interviews is the struggle with **Confidence**. 
- Users are often misled by "structural clarity" and "authoritative tone."
- *Counter-measure*: Expert users have learned to "ignore overly confident wording" and "politeness" to focus on raw data.

## Theme 2: Contextual Verification (The "Stakes" Gradient)
Verification is not a constant behavior but a variable one.
- **Low Stakes**: Brainstorming, social media, daily help (Lower verification).
- **High Stakes**: Tax reviews, medical/legal advice, academic assignments, coding architecture (Highest verification).

## Theme 3: The "Echo Chamber" Effect
Users are becoming aware that AI often prioritizes "agreeing with the user" over "being correct."
- *Insight*: Shwetlana specifically double-checks when she feels the AI is just mirroring her prompt.

## Theme 4: The "Anti-Fluff" Sentiment
There is a clear pattern of users ignoring "lengthy descriptions" and "elaborative" text.
- *Insight*: Users perceive verbosity as a potential mask for weak reasoning or hallucinations.

## Theme 5: Source-Based Legitimacy
Citations are viewed as the ultimate "Trust Anchor."
- However, the research shows a gap: users want citations, but they also want the system to prove those citations aren't hallucinated.

## Theme 6: Human Accountability (The "FDM" Role)
Regardless of AI capability, users consistently view themselves as the "Final Decision Maker."
- Subhojyoti and Sanchita emphasize the need to "double check the whole output" regardless of how confident the AI sounds.
