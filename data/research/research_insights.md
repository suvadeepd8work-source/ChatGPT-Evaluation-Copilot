# Research Insights: ChatGPT Evaluation Mode

## Executive Summary
Analysis of 7 user interviews and 3 core personas reveals a fundamental tension: users value AI for efficiency but are increasingly wary of "confident hallucinations." There is a strong desire for a system that doesn't just provide answers, but provides **proof**.

## Key Findings
- **The "Confidence" Fallacy**: 100% of student personas and 71% of interviewees admitted to trusting AI simply because it "sounds professional" or "confident," even when they later found it to be wrong.
- **Verification is Task-Dependent**: Users only double-check when the stakes are high (Assignments, Work Reports, Tax Returns, Financial Decisions). Low-stakes tasks (Brainstorming, Social Media) go almost entirely unverified.
- **Source Hunger**: The most requested feature across all segments is "verified sources" and "built-in fact-checking." Users are tired of "fake citations" (Neha persona).
- **The "Fluff" Filter**: Experienced users (Subhojyoti, Debapriya) have learned to ignore "too elaborative" or "lengthy" descriptions, viewing them as low-value or potentially hiding errors.

## User Pain Points
1. **Hallucinations**: Confident but incorrect facts (Soumya, Navarup).
2. **Fake Citations**: Invented books or links that don't exist (Neha).
3. **Echo-Chambering**: AI agreeing with the user's prompt rather than being correct (Shwetlana).
4. **Weak Reasoning**: Logic that looks sound at a glance but fails under scrutiny (Sanchita).

## Desired Outcomes
- **Transparency**: Knowing *where* the information came from.
- **Accountability**: A clear understanding that the human is still in charge.
- **Speed with Safety**: Not having to spend more time verifying than the AI saved them in generating.
